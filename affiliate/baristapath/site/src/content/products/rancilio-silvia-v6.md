The Rancilio Silvia has been in production with minor revisions since 1997, and the V6 keeps the formula: commercial 58mm group head, brass boiler, iron frame, articulating wand. It is heavier and more thermally stable than the Gaggia Classic Pro, at the cost of price and warm-up time.

Out of the box, Silvia behaves like a smaller version of a cafe machine. That includes its quirks: no PID means you learn temperature surfing or you install a Silvia PID kit. Most enthusiasts choose the latter within a year.

If you want to spend once and forget about the machine for a decade, Silvia is one of the few options at this price that earns that promise. Pair with a dedicated grinder (Silvia Pro versions add a second boiler but cost considerably more — the V6 here is the classic single-boiler).
