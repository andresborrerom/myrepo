The Rattleware milk thermometer is the cheapest reliable solution to one of the most common beginner mistakes in milk steaming: overheating. Milk denatures above 70C (158F) and develops a scalded flavor; the specialty coffee target is 60-65C (140-149F) for latte and cappuccino. Beginners without temperature reference routinely push past 70C, producing thin and slightly-bitter milk that ruins the drink.

The Rattleware design is the standard analog dial thermometer used across most home and small-cafe setups. A 1.5-inch dial sits at the top of a 5-inch stainless probe; a clip secures the thermometer to the side of a milk pitcher with a pre-drilled mount hole (Bellman 20oz, some Joe Frex sizes). The probe drops into the milk and the dial reads in both Fahrenheit and Celsius across a wide range.

The trade-off is analog lag. The dial physically rotates as the bimetallic strip inside the probe heats — this happens 2-3 seconds behind the actual milk temperature, so users need to stop steaming a few degrees before the target reading. For the first one to three months of microfoam practice, this is fine — the goal is to develop a sense of when milk hits 60-65C by hand-feel on the pitcher exterior. Most users remove the thermometer after that and rely on touch; it remains useful as a backup or for users who steam infrequently and never fully internalize the hand-feel signal.
