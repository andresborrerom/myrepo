A puck screen is a thin disc of stainless steel mesh placed on top of the tamped coffee puck before the portafilter locks into the group head. Its job is to spread incoming preinfusion water evenly across the bed rather than letting a single jet from the shower screen punch a hole into the puck — which is the most common channeling cause on light roasts and finely-ground espresso.

The Normcore 58.5mm puck screen is the value pick in this category. At $19 it is half the price of the equivalent Saint Anthony Industries or Bplus screens, with 90% of the effect. The five-layer 150-micron 316 stainless mesh is the right specification for espresso — fine enough to spread water but coarse enough not to clog within a week. The 58.5mm diameter (with 0.5mm clearance against the basket wall) is the same fit as a 58mm commercial tamper.

The honest assessment of puck screens is that they produce a small effect. Users running a Specialita or Niche-class grinder with proper WDT and an even tamp do not need one. The screen matters more for users with channeling issues — common on light roasts, common on freshly-roasted beans, common with grinders that produce uneven distributions. The second benefit is shower screen cleanliness: a puck screen catches the fines that normally splash up into the group, which extends the maintenance interval and reduces the frequency of full disassembly cleanings.
