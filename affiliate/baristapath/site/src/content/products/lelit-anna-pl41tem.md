The Lelit Anna PL41TEM is one of the more honest entry-prosumer machines in the segment: a compact single-boiler design with PID temperature control and a standard 57mm portafilter. It targets the user who has outgrown a thermoblock starter but isn't ready to spend $1,500 on an E61 dual boiler.

PID stability is the headline. The Anna holds brew temperature within roughly a degree once warmed up, which matters more for shot consistency than most spec-sheet items at this price. The three-way solenoid valve is also worth flagging — it relieves pressure off the puck so the spent grounds knock out as a dry disk rather than a wet slurry.

Workflow compromises are real. As a single boiler, the Anna brews and steams from the same heat source, so milk drinks require a temperature surf between modes. For espresso-forward users who drink mostly straight shots or americanos, that limitation is invisible. For latte-heavy households, step up to a heat exchanger or dual boiler.
