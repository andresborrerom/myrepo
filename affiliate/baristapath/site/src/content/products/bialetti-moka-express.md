The Bialetti Moka Express is the most recognized coffee brewer in Italian kitchens. Invented in 1933 by Alfonso Bialetti and still produced in Italy today, the Moka Express works on a different principle than espresso machines — steam pressure (around 1.5 bar) pushes hot water through a coffee bed rather than the 9-bar pressure of true espresso.

The output is not espresso, despite frequent comparisons. It's concentrated, dark, full-bodied stovetop coffee that works well as a base for milk drinks (Italian-style breakfast caffé latte) or diluted with hot water for an americano. For users who grew up with this style or who want concentrated coffee without buying an espresso machine, the Moka is the canonical tool.

Technique matters. Heat must be moderate — high heat scorches the bed and produces bitter, burnt cups. Grind size should be medium-fine, finer than drip but coarser than espresso. Aluminum bodies need hand-washing (not dishwasher) to preserve the patina that develops over use. For under $40, the Moka Express is one of the highest-value brewers in coffee.
