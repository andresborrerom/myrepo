eSun TPU 95A is the most common entry point for users moving from rigid filaments (PLA, PETG) into flexibles. The 95A Shore-A hardness is the practical sweet spot — soft enough to be useful for cases and gaskets, firm enough that retraction tuning is manageable. Softer TPU (85A, 75A) gets harder to extrude reliably and effectively requires direct-drive.

Print speed is the trade-off. TPU does not feed reliably at PLA / PETG speeds; expect 20-40mm/s for clean results, which means a part that prints in 1 hour in PLA may take 3-4 hours in TPU. Bowden extruders will need extended retraction tuning to avoid blobbing; direct-drive extruders make TPU dramatically more tractable.

Moisture is the other gotcha: TPU absorbs water faster than most filaments, and a print from a wet spool will visibly pop and string in ways the same spool wouldn't fresh from the bag. Plan for a dry-box workflow or a small filament dryer if you'll use TPU regularly.
